Who I Am

As a software engineer, I live at the intersection of logic and physical hardware, where a line of code has to end in something that actually moves, senses, or decides. That pull started early and hasn't let up: from coaching robotics teams to digitizing complex operational workflows to co-founding a hardware startup, the throughline is always the same question, how do you turn intelligence into something built?

I care more about foundational thinking than shortcuts. Whether I'm teaching a student their first line of logic or architecting an access-control system, I'd rather build systems that are understood from the ground up than something that just works and can't be explained.
